# Visual music: my ideas

## The big idea

- Visual art for classical performance, starting with solo flute. Some effects are written ahead for a piece, some are spontaneous.
- Not AI generated for now.
- Rather than another generic reactive app (there are lots already), a set of visuals made for a specific piece. Someone can look at your visuals, use them, and tweak them.

## Where it runs

- Vision Pro: the audience wears it, not the performer. One listener and one performer for now. The listener sees the real performer and the room.
- Laptop too, since not everyone has a headset. Front camera with butterflies around your head on its own feels like a really basic app.
- Starts as a playground project in the lab, not inside the flute studio.

## Phrases, not events

- Visuals respond to phrases, but not in a rigid "a phrase comes, a phrase goes" way. They should follow how we naturally experience music.
- A phrase: loudness goes up, then down, then there's a rest where you breathe.
- First phrase of Syrinx: starts soft, gets a little louder, gets softer and disappears. The pitch goes down chromatically, returns to the first note, and holds the last note a little.

## Mapping ideas

- A line effect, even something that simple, can be beautiful.
- Pitch as height makes literal sense but not musical sense. Pitch as color instead.
- Loudness as how fast the line moves forward.
- It keeps moving in one direction, left to right, but rotates a little, so it feels like a swirl of wind or particles, not a single 2D line.
- Color could follow a gradient, or play with harmony: the tonic has a color, the 7th has a color, the leading tone has its own, chromatic notes get a texture.
- To express it emotionally, analyze the music: major, minor, pentatonic, whole-tone and so on.
- Dynamics are written in the music, so the visuals don't have to depend on raw decibels, which won't be that reliable live with noise.
- Map timing from a recording of the piece, then use timing plus pitch to know where we are while playing. Different performers play a bit differently, but not by much.

## Syrinx and the story

- Come from the story, the Greek myth of Pan and his flute, not just from showing the chords.
- A butterfly that lands on your finger.
- Wind swirling around your body.
- The performer could wear a hat, like Pan.
- Agreed analysis angles: the ABA' form, scale types per passage, the tritone, and musical forces (gravity, magnetism, inertia).
- To watch: Bernstein's lecture "The Delights and Dangers of Ambiguity" (The Unanswered Question), on Debussy's Faune.

## Side project: staccato raindrops

- Listen for staccatos and turn them into raindrops that fade in and out at random places on the screen.
- Examples, not literal rules: a soft note is a faint drop, a harsh one is a stone, a long gentle one is a feather.
- What to listen for: pitch, volume, attack (harder or softer), length (staccato can be short or long), and ending (abrupt or tapered).
- Different kinds of staccato: aggressive, secco, détaché.
- Clean, minimal screen: no titles, subtitles or explanations.

## Separate project: a visual guide for students

- Animation on the body for teaching.
- A high soft note shows fast air; a change in tone color shows as a color change.
